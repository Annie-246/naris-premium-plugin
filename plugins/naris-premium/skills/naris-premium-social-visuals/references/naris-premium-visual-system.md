# Naris Premium visual system

This document distills the supplied Naris Premium brand guideline for social-post production. The original PDF remains in `naris-premium-brand-guideline.pdf` for visual verification.

## Brand character

Every concept should express four qualities together:

- **Phụ nữ (feminine):** graceful, composed, tactile, beauty-led; never childish or sugary.
- **Tinh tế (refined):** selective detail, generous space, quiet confidence, and deliberate hierarchy.
- **Khoa học (scientific):** precision, clean structure, credible cosmetic cues, and controlled material rendering; not a clinical hospital aesthetic.
- **Sang trọng (luxurious):** premium restraint, editorial composition, warm neutrals, subtle depth, and high-quality light rather than flashy effects.

The source guideline also frames Naris Cosmetic as a long-established Japanese beauty brand founded in 1932, guided by “For Others,” with an emphasis on safe, gentle products and customer trust. Use this only as brand context. Do not turn it into advertising copy or a claim unless the user supplies or approves that wording.

## Color palette

### Primary colors

- Warm ivory: `#FAF6F3` - C 1%, M 2%, Y 3%, K 0%.
- Deep cocoa brown: `#51362B` - C 49%, M 68%, Y 73%, K 54%.

Use warm ivory as the principal light field and deep cocoa for high-contrast text, anchors, or dark fields. The design should usually feel warm, quiet, and tonal rather than stark black-and-white.

### Secondary colors

- Light greige: `#D4CABF` - C 16%, M 17%, Y 22%, K 0%.
- Taupe: `#A4907B` - C 36%, M 40%, Y 51%, K 4%.
- Dark taupe-brown: `#574436` - C 52%, M 61%, Y 71%, K 45%.
- Near-black brown: `#392B1F` - C 52%, M 61%, Y 71%, K 70%.

Build tonal depth within this family. Natural product colors may remain accurate, but supporting graphics and environments should harmonize with the palette. Avoid dominant neon, candy, electric, or cold synthetic colors unless the user explicitly asks for a controlled campaign exception.

## Typography

- Default headline face: **Editorial New Ultra Light**.
- **SVN-Aptima** is an approved alternative only when the user explicitly requests it.
- Supporting/body face: **Roboto**.

The plugin bundles the approved font assets below. Use the bundled files instead of searching for substitutes when the production workflow supports deterministic typesetting:

- Editorial New Vietnamese Ultra Light and Ultra Light Italic: `assets/fonts/editorial-new/`.
- SVN-Aptima Regular, Italic, Bold, and Bold Italic: `assets/fonts/svn-aptima/`.
- Roboto variable Roman and Italic: `assets/fonts/roboto/`, together with its OFL license.

The project owner confirmed permission to package and share the supplied Editorial New and SVN-Aptima files in this Naris Premium plugin. Keep those files scoped to this plugin; do not publish or redistribute them separately.

Do not use GPT Image to draw required promotional typography. Generate a clean art plate, then typeset the headline from `BHN-Editorial-New-Ultra-Light.otf` and supporting copy from the bundled Roboto variable font. Do not approximate these fonts visually. If the user explicitly requests SVN-Aptima, use the corresponding bundled file rather than synthesizing the style.

Keep typography sparse, poised, and legible. Use scale, whitespace, and alignment before decorative effects. Avoid bubbly fonts, heavy condensed display faces, tech/sci-fi lettering, loud outlines, thick drop shadows, excessive all-caps, or multiple competing styles.

## Logo

The approved social lockup is the Naris emblem in a square followed by the Naris wordmark and `JAPAN`. Use the bundled black or white transparent PNG according to contrast.

- Keep the original proportions and orientation.
- Do not stretch, redraw, recolor, outline, bevel, shadow, crop, or separate the emblem from the lockup.
- Do not ask the image model to spell or recreate the logo from memory.
- Maintain clear space around the lockup. As a practical social-post minimum, keep surrounding space at least comparable to the height of the square emblem and increase it when the composition permits.
- Prefer the black logo on warm ivory or pale neutral fields and the white logo on deep brown or sufficiently dark photographic fields.
- Keep the logo subordinate to the product and headline but clearly readable.

## Imagery and materials

Favor imagery that feels feminine, refined, scientific, and premium:

- soft directional or diffused light;
- cosmetic textures, glass, translucent liquids, polished or satin surfaces;
- botanical details used selectively;
- warm spa interiors or Japanese-inspired quietness without clichés;
- clean laboratory cues such as precision glassware only when relevant;
- gentle shadows, subtle reflections, tonal gradients, and restrained depth.

Avoid busy prop styling, kitschy Japanese motifs, generic luxury gold overload, hard nightclub lighting, exaggerated splashes, excessive particles, fantasy ingredients, or unsupported scientific diagrams.

## Composition for social posts

The guideline examples use calm neutral fields, generous negative space, small logo placement, and compact editorial copy anchored near an edge or lower area. Treat these as principles, not a mandatory template.

- Establish one dominant focal area, normally the product.
- Keep the headline secondary but clear.
- Use large quiet regions to signal refinement.
- Align elements to a simple grid; avoid many floating badges or cards.
- Let one meaningful material, botanical, scientific, or lifestyle cue support the product instead of surrounding it with unrelated objects.
- At thumbnail size, the product should remain identifiable and the headline should retain a clear entry point.

## Brand anti-patterns

Reject outputs that feel mass-market, crowded, cute, discount-first, hyper-glossy, neon, overly clinical, generically “Asian,” or assembled from reference fragments. Also reject outputs with fake seals, unverified statistics, random Japanese characters, decorative microtext, repeated logos, or altered product packaging.
